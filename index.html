The problem

What manufacturers are struggling with in technician hiring today

The people who know how to run these plants are retiring, and the people replacing them have never done the job. Every conversation I’d have starts from one of these six problems. The quotes are illustrative of what plant and HR leaders say.

The retiring workforce and the knowledge cliff

“Dave has run this line for 31 years and retires in March. Nobody else knows why we do half of what we do.”

Where Ava fits: Scenarios built from what your best technicians know, so every new hire is screened against the standard they set, and training time goes to plant-specific knowledge.

“Every applicant says they can troubleshoot a conveyor. Half of them can’t.”

03

Prospect & contact

Let the executive take the interview

The fastest way to sell Ava is to let a buyer meet her. Every sequence carries one move: a four-minute Ava assessment link the executive or plant leader can take on their phone. After that, the meeting is about their numbers, not about whether AI interviewing works.

DAY 1

Warm intro or a short email to the CHRO

Tied to the public signal: their ramp, their hiring number, their technician roles. One ask: 20 minutes with their TA lead.

DAY 2

LinkedIn note to the TA leader and plant leader

Different message for each. The TA leader hears about recruiter hours; the plant leader hears about who reaches the floor.

DAY 4

“Take the interview yourself”

A four-minute Ava assessment built from one of their own public job descriptions. This is the move that gets forwarded.

DAY 7

Phone call

To the plant or TA leader. Short, one question: what happens to a technician candidate between applying and the first day on the floor?

DAY 10

Proof from a peer

A short customer result from a comparable manufacturer, with the founders’ approval, or an invitation to a small industry roundtable.

DAY 14

Close the loop

A polite last note that leaves the assessment link open. Accounts that go quiet return to nurture with their next public signal.

04

Discovery

Leave the first call with a baseline

Discovery has one job: collect the numbers the pilot will be judged against. If we can’t agree on the “before,” nobody can prove the “after,” and the pilot never converts.

Volume & speed

  1. How many technician hires this year, and at which sites?Sizes the deal and picks the pilot site.
  2. How many applicants does it take to make one hire?Sets the screening load Ava removes.
  3. How many days from application to start, and where does it stall?Finds the bottleneck to attack first.

Quality & turnover

  1. What share of new technicians leave or wash out in the first 90 days?The number the CFO cares about.
  2. What does a mis-hire cost you once training seats and overtime are counted?Turns turnover into dollars.
  3. How do hiring managers decide someone can do the work today?Surfaces the gap Ava fills.

Process & stack

  1. Which ATS, and who owns it?Workday, SuccessFactors or iCIMS; brings IT in early.
  2. What assessments run today, and who wrote them?Finds the incumbent or the spreadsheet.
  3. Are any of these roles covered by a union agreement?Changes who has to be in the room.

Risk & decision

  1. Who reviews AI tools used in hiring, and what do they need to see?California’s automated-decision rules took effect Oct 1, 2025.
  2. Who signs a paid pilot, and who signs the enterprise contract?Rarely the same person.
  3. If the pilot hits its numbers, what happens next, and by when?Agree on conversion before kickoff.

The baseline I leave discovery with

  • Technician hires per year, per site
  • Applicants per hire
  • Recruiter minutes per screen
  • Days from application to start
  • 90-day turnover rate
  • Cost of one early leaver
  • Named exec sponsor and pilot owner
  • Written definition of pilot success
05

Paid pilot

One plant, one role family, success written down

A pilot that isn’t paid, scoped and scored is a free trial. I’d structure every pilot the same way so it converts on evidence, and so the logo lands on signature.

Scope

Narrow on purpose

One site and one role family, such as maintenance technicians at one fab or assemblers on one line. Big enough to measure, small enough to launch in weeks.

Terms

Paid, 60 to 90 days

A fixed pilot fee credited toward year one. Conversion pricing and the enterprise paper agreed before kickoff, so success triggers a signature, not a new negotiation.

Scorecard

Three numbers

Time-to-hire, hiring-manager rating of new hires, and 90-day retention against the discovery baseline. A midpoint review at day 30 with the exec sponsor.

What this shows, and what it doesn’t

  • Gross value, not ROI. TAP’s price is not subtracted. Net return comes after pricing is set.
  • Hours returned are capacity. They become cash savings only when they reduce agency fees, overtime or planned recruiter hires.
  • TAP’s results are applied conservatively. 89% is TAP’s published reduction in assessment time. TAP publishes a 16% retention improvement; the model reads it as 16% fewer early leavers.
  • Every other input is a sample. Discovery replaces them with the customer’s own numbers, and the pilot replaces the 89% and 16% with measured results.
